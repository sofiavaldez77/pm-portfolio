Case Study 01

From "This Is Definitely a Scam" to 2.5x Conversion

How I translated usability interviews of Panda Mobile's ad-to-onboarding flow into a redesign that drove 2.5x checkout conversion.

CompanyPanda Mobile · Moxee Technologies
Timeline< 2 months, part-time

Panda Mobile users, on the original onboarding funnel
The Ask

A new mobile carrier with a 96% drop-off problem

Panda Mobile is a new online mobile carrier (powered by the T-Mobile network). At the time, they had a live product, live ads, and real users. But only 4.67% of checkout-page visitors converted.

I was asked to redesign the onboarding funnel to double that conversion rate in under two months.

Background Research

Identifying the current users, the highest-traffic path, and the team constraints

01
The current users

The target audience was undefinedStakeholders and marketing were weighing international travelers vs. international Chinese students as the target audience., and there was no profile of the current users.

→ I defined the current user profile based on the acquisition messaging ($15 to $20 early-access pricing) and the newness of the brand:

Price-conscious · Primary account holders · Early adopters
Panda Mobile's highest-traffic acquisition paths

→ I aligned with stakeholders to design for current users, since the funnel would also serve other Moxee brands with similar profiles.

Early hypothesis
A major part of the drop-off stems from an intent mismatch: the ad implies a waitlist, but leads to checkout. Interviews should cover the full ad-to-onboarding path, not just the onboarding funnel.
03
The team constraints
  • Engineering (6 people) supports 5 Moxee mobile brands → small team, limited bandwidth → prioritize implementation simplicity
  • While engineering owns early access and onboarding, marketing owns ads and the landing page → worth collaborating with marketing for a cohesive experience

Customer Interviews

7 interviews. My hypothesis held up by the third.

  • 7 participants matching Panda's current user profile+
    • Relative: Male, 60s, AT&T
    • Relatives: Married couple, 20s, T-Mobile
    • Friend: Male, 20s, Verizon
    • Friend: Male, 20s, Mint
    • Stranger (park): Male, 20s, T-Mobile
    • Stranger (social media): Female, 20s, Spectrum
  • 1 hr Zoom sessions: participants thought aloud through the highest-traffic path; neutral follow-up questions only.
01
Users expected a waitlist. They got a checkout page.
  • By the third interview, the pattern was clear: the ad set an expectation of more information, not payment, triggering confusion and distrust.

"Now they want my payment? I'm confused. What am I paying for? I thought this was going to be a waitlist."

"I expect a link sent to my email but instead see a button to set up my service."

"You're in? What am I in?"

"I just paid for something. I guess I have to check my email to see what just paid for."

"Did I purchase a plan or registration fee?"

  • I proposed two options to the VP of Product:
    1) Redesign the early access funnel into a truer waitlist experience
    2) Remove the early access funnel entirely and route new ads straight to the landing page → selected, due to a parallel marketing decision
Updated interview flow

02
Confusion and distrust showed up at every stage, not just onboarding

All interviews confirmed this called for a full ad-to-onboarding redesign.

→ 1) define system-level customer needs, and 2) collaborate with marketing on the ads and landing page.
