## Block 2: The Core Insight
This is the top-level insight driving the entire strategy — state it plainly, up front, before the word-of-mouth-bubble detail:

**This audience is deal/discount-motivated — they need to feel like they're getting their plan "at a deal."** But motivation alone isn't enough to make them switch. Two conditions both have to be true:
1. They hear about the option from inside their own word-of-mouth bubble (the primary way this audience discovers and trusts a carrier).
2. The price is either much lower, OR comparable *and* stacked with valuable features/discounts on top.

**Word-of-mouth bubble (tooltip/expandable "?"):** Each student's carrier options and sense of what counts as "cheap" or "reliable" are shaped by the specific network of friends, classmates, and community groups (often CSSA/WeChat-based) they're plugged into — not by independent research or advertising.

**Source:** Slide 41. Quotes to pull in in the expandable: interview quotes on carrier discovery via friends/WeChat groups and price-driven switching (already sourced in earlier outline — reuse from Sections 5 "Word-of-Mouth" and 6a "Family Plans" quote banks).

## Block 3: The Three Word-of-Mouth Bubbles
From talking to the audience about their own carrier history and their friends', three low-price word-of-mouth bubbles emerged in the competitive landscape:

1. **Chinese carrier (ENC)** — massive first-year discount
2. **Big Wireless (T-Mobile, etc.)** — family plan discount
3. **Mint Mobile** — consistently low price/discounts

**Source:** Slide 41, competitive positioning slides (39–41 range already sourced from the deck read).

---

## Block 4: The Strategic Choice
Stakeholders explicitly did not want to compete on lowest price — this is a **cost leadership vs. differentiation** decision (Porter's generic strategies: racing to be the cheapest option isn't sustainable for a startup competing against carriers with far more scale, so the strategic choice was to differentiate instead).

Given the audience is price-sensitive but only within their own bubble, the question became: which of the three bubbles could Panda realistically match on price *and* clearly differentiate against?

**Answer: Big Wireless family plan users.** They're already paying a comparable price (~$35/mo), already dissatisfied (family plan hassle), and Panda could offer things Big Wireless structurally couldn't easily match: Chinese-community features, individual-plan flexibility, and a startup's ability to move fast on a cheap, high-value feature.

**Source:** Slides 39–41.

## Block 5: Plan of Attack

### 5a. Base: Comparable Price
**$35/mo unlimited plan** — matches what Big Wireless family plan users are already paying. This removes price as a switching barrier before any other value prop is introduced.

### 5b. Plan Value Props (three sub-areas)

**i. No Family Plan (direct win + engineering bonus)**
- Individual plans only, no family plan required.
- Direct win vs. T-Mobile: audience insight showed they wanted *out* of family plans, not into them — the hassle (deposits, unpaid bills, non-account-holder friction) outweighed the savings.
- Bonus: engineering was already planning to build family plan support as a major investment. This recommendation gave them a reason to cut that investment entirely rather than build it.

**ii. Chinese Features (ranked in this order)**
1. **Pause your plan** — ranked #1 by the audience, ~2-day engineering effort. Directly contrasted against the food delivery partnership stakeholders were considering, which would also have been a massive investment yet ranked #16 of 22 evaluated features.
2. **SIM card** — ranked #2. Engineering was planning to prioritize eSIM, but this audience skews toward needing physical SIM (many arrive with China-purchased phones incompatible with eSIM) — recommendation was to deprioritize eSIM investment for this segment.
3. **Mandarin-speaking support** — already existed; kept and reinforced as a differentiator.
4. **Dual number OR limited international calling** — dual # ranked #7 and was already being discussed by stakeholders; international calling ranked #16 and was being discussed as *unlimited*, but the recommendation was to scale it back — audience primarily uses WhatsApp/WeChat over WiFi to reach family, and only needs calling for emergencies, so unlimited was unnecessary investment.

**iii. Brand Positioning**
Chinese-catered brand positioning, tying the above together (see slide 43 for full elaboration). This is where the current Panda Mobile blog (https://pandamobile.com/blogs/) should be referenced as evidence this positioning took hold and continued — worth its own small callout/link in this sub-block.

**Slogan callout:** Sofia coined the phrase **"The mobile carrier that travels with you"** — Panda Mobile has since adopted it as their tagline (visible in the footer of the current landing page). This is a strong, concrete, ownable proof point and should get its own distinct callout in this sub-block — not buried in the general brand-positioning paragraph. Treat it similarly to the checklist items elsewhere in this section: a clear "I coined this" claim, clickable/highlightable to the exact spot it appears on the landing page footer.

### 5c. Marketing (two sub-areas)

**i. Incentive/deal-motivation push**
- A "special" student discount on top of the already-comparable price and value props — kept deliberately small, since price was already comparable and the value props did most of the work (contrast with ENC's massive first-year discount, which wasn't sustainable and wasn't necessary here).
- Referral incentive marketed aggressively ("unlimited savings," uncapped referrals) — serves two purposes: it's a switching incentive for the deal-motivated audience, and it drives word-of-mouth spread, which is the audience's primary discovery channel.

**ii. Getting directly into the word-of-mouth bubble**
- The "Bye Bye Family Plan" campaign, targeted specifically at T-Mobile family plan users.
- CSSA/community presence (slide 45) — direct engagement with the same community networks that already act as the audience's word-of-mouth bubble.
- Referral program again reinforced here as the mechanism that drives spread once Panda is inside the bubble.

**Source:** Slides 43, 45.
