Case Study 02

What Started as Landing Page Testing Ended Up Shaping a Brand

How interviews meant to test landing page variations ended up reshaping product roadmap, marketing strategy, and brand positioning instead.

RoleAssociate Product Manager (solo)
CompanyPanda Mobile · Moxee Technologies
Timeline< 2 months, part-time
Team6 product/eng · 4 marketing
The Ask

A New Target Audience. I Offered to Test the Landing Page With Users.

Panda Mobile is a new online mobile carrier, powered by the T-Mobile network. Marketing was testing new brand elements (color themes, a mascot, slogan options) on the landing page for the new audience.

→ I offered to interview target users on the variations.

Recruitment

From 1 Contact to 55 Sign-Ups Across 10+ States

Survey results hinted the segment was deal-motivated, so I designed a referral incentive: $15 for a 1-hour interview, plus $10 per successful referral, up to 5.

55
Sign-ups for interviews, within 3 days

From 1 person I knew in Indiana, our network grew to span 10+ states & 3 countries!

Already a sign of what this audience valued: word-of-mouth, and an incentive to spread it.

The Pivot
Early Insights Were Already Contradicting Survey Data

Example: survey data suggested the audience wanted family plans, a major planned engineering investment. Interviews said otherwise: people found them a hassle, staying mainly for the price or word-of-mouth.

→ I proposed swapping the landing page questions for price/feature exploration.

One Executive Doubted I Was Equipped to Run This Alone

He'd come from T-Mobile, a company with a well-resourced research team, and didn't think one early-career product manager was equipped to collect the right research alone.

I Looped In Every Stakeholder Directly
  • 1:1 with the executive: walked through my plan, agreed the presentation would stay primarily insights, with any recommendations previewed to him first.
  • 1:1 with every other stakeholder too, building their questions into the plan.
03
The Next 15 Interviews

I went more in depth on carrier history and switching motivations, and added 2 exercises: a mobile plan feature ranking and a pricing/feature sensitivity analysis for plans.

The Deliverable

A Company-Wide Presentation: Mostly Insights, Strategy at the End

Thematic analysis surfaced 21 quote-supported insights on challenges, community, carrier history & priorities, and plan/feature sensitivity.

→ Insight-informed strategy came last and stayed intentionally low-effort, sized to the team's engineering bandwidth and framed to earn stakeholder buy-in rather than dictate it.

02
The Core Insight: Target Users Will Only Switch to Panda Mobile If…
1. It Reaches Them Through Word-of-Mouth

Carrier choice, especially the first one, primarily comes from inside their own networkEach student's carrier options, and sense of what counts as "cheap" or "reliable," are shaped by the specific network of friends, classmates, and community groups (often CSSA/WeChat-based) they're plugged into, not by independent research or advertising.

2. The Price Is a "Deal"

Much lower, or comparable and stacked with a discount and value props

Plan of Attack: Here's How We Use What We Already Have to Compete With T-Mobile
Base
Product
↓
Comparable Price Without the Family Plan

$35/mo individual unlimited plan option

  • Target users didn't want to be on family plans in the first place
  • Panda already had individual plans, and could cut the planned family-plan feature that would be a major engineering investment
Chinese-Community Value Props
  • RecommendedImplement a "Pause your plan" feature: ranked #1 in interviews and would be a ~2-day build, vs. the planned food delivery partnership ranked #16 that would be a massive investment.
  • RecommendedLimit unlimited international data, ranked #15 in interviews since this audience primarily calls home over WeChat on WiFi, and invest in a dual number instead, ranked #7.
  • RecommendedMandarin-speaking support, already in place and ranked #13 in interviews, but 24/7 support ranked #3, room to grow.
