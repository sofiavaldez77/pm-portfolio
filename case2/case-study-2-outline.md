## Section 3: The Pivot and the Pushback
**Working headline:** *Three Interviews In, I Asked to Change the Scope. One Executive Said No.*

**Content:** Three interviews in, Sofia recognized the conversations held insights well beyond brand-theme testing — live product and marketing decisions for this new audience were being debated (including a planned food delivery partnership and plan pricing). She proposed widening the interview focus to carrier history, switching motivations, and a feature/price tradeoff analysis, dropping landing-page questions to make room. A C-suite executive pushed back, concerned that a single early-career researcher would introduce bias into decisions he felt needed a more resourced research team (he referenced his prior experience at T-Mobile). Resolution: a 1:1 meeting where Sofia walked him through her interview plan, incorporated the specific features/prices he wanted evaluated, and agreed to keep the final presentation insights-first rather than recommendation-heavy, with a preview to him before the company-wide readout.

## Section 6: Redirected — What They Planned vs. What I Found
**Working headline:** *They Were Building One Thing. My Interviews Pointed to Another.*

This section should contain two full insight → redirect beats, told individually, not compressed:

### 6a. Family Plans
**Fact:** Survey data had suggested adding family plans as a feature — a major engineering investment, since Chinese students were seen on family plans already.
**What interviews found:** Students joined family plans for price, but genuinely hated the hassle.
**Quote (verbatim):** *"I think the required deposit about $500 or so to join the family plan. It was just a lot of hassle. I didn't know anyone at the time, either, so it was hard for me to join public credit plan."*
**Redirect:** Instead of building family plans, Sofia recommended the "Bye Bye Family Plan" campaign — positioning Panda's absence of family plans as the value, competing directly at Big Wireless's family-plan price point.

### 6b. Pause Plan vs. Food Delivery
**Fact:** A food delivery partnership was already being planned/discussed — a multi-month, expensive engineering lift.
**What interviews found:** Food delivery ranked low in feature prioritization (1 must-have, 5 nice-to-have, 6 don't-care mentions). "Pause your plan" ranked #1 — participants said they'd get the plan just for that feature — and was estimated at a 2-day engineering effort.
**Redirect:** Sofia recommended prioritizing pause-plan over the food delivery partnership, redirecting engineering effort toward a cheap, high-value feature instead of an expensive, low-ranked one.
**Note:** Unlimited international data can be folded in here as a smaller supporting beat — also planned as a feature, also found low-priority (students primarily use WhatsApp over WiFi; unlimited data seen as necessary only for emergencies).

**Evidence needed:** Both beats are fully sourced from the deck (slides on family plans, feature prioritization, and the competitive positioning/"Bye Bye Family Plan" slides). No additional assets needed for this section.
